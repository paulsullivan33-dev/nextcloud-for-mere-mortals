# The Neighbours

**Scope:** what else could hold your
files and calendars — the commercial
clouds, the other self-hosted tools in
and out of this series — and what each
one trades against Nextcloud.

## The commercial clouds

**Dropbox, Google Drive, iCloud,
OneDrive** sell the same shape without
the server: sign up, sync, share. What
they trade away is the point of this
guide — the files live on someone
else's computers, under someone else's
terms, bill, and reading of the law.
What they trade *for* it is real:
backups you don't run, upgrades you
don't schedule, and a support line.
For a household that values its
evenings over its custody, they are a
respectable answer. The honest
comparison is not "cloud bad" — it is
"whose machine, whose rules, whose
problem at midnight."

## The self-hosted alternatives

| Neighbour | What it is | The trade |
|---|---|---|
| **Syncthing** | pure folder sync between your machines, no server | no web page, no accounts, no links — and nothing to administer; this series has a guide |
| **Seafile** | a sync-and-share server, leaner and faster-feeling | smaller community, files stored in its own block format — the data folder is not just files |
| **Immich** | the photo specialist of this series | does photos and video superbly; doesn't pretend to be a filing cabinet |
| **A NAS's own suite** (Synology Drive and kin) | the box you already own, offering the same shape | convenience now, the vendor's roadmap and lifespan later |
| **ownCloud** | Nextcloud's parent project, still maintained | the two share ancestry and differ in direction and licensing weather; read both projects' current state before choosing |

Two patterns in the table are worth
more than the rows. First, the
**just-files test**: Nextcloud and
Syncthing keep your files as files on
a disk you can read without them; the
moment a tool stores your life in a
private format, your exit gets
expensive, and you should price that
in on day one. Second, **specialists
beat suites at their specialty** —
Immich's photo handling is better
than Nextcloud's, Syncthing's sync is
more single-minded — while the suite
wins on having one server, one
account list, and one backup to run.

## When Nextcloud is the wrong door

- **Photos are all you keep** → Immich,
  and visit its guide in this series.
- **You only want folders kept in
  step, no web anything** → Syncthing.
- **Nobody in the house will ever run
  an upgrade** → a commercial cloud,
  honestly chosen, beats a self-hosted
  server abandoned at version-minus-six.
- **The files must be unreadable by
  the server itself** → none of these;
  that's end-to-end encrypted storage,
  a different shelf of tools with its
  own costs in convenience.

Check: you can say, for the neighbour
nearest your situation, what it does
better than Nextcloud and what it
trades away — and the phrase "just
files" means something testable to you
now. Chapter 14 turns the same honesty
on Nextcloud itself.
